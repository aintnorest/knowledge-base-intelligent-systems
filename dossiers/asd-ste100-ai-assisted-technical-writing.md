# ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English and Artificial Intelligence — Study Notes

**Authoring body**: ASD Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group (STEMG) and Artificial Intelligence Task Team  
**Date**: June 2026  
**Pages**: 3

## What It Is

This white paper states the STEMG position on AI-assisted drafting, translation, and management of ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English. It accepts that LLMs can accelerate production, terminology work, compliance checking, and multilingual consistency, but makes the standard—not a tool's output—the controlling authority. STEMG does not endorse AI products and extends its existing software and training non-endorsement policy to them.

## Operating Position

AI is supportive only. Human authors and organizations retain responsibility for accuracy, safety, and compliance, especially because model output can be factually unreliable, weaken terminology control, obscure sources, and mishandle proprietary material. The paper calls for continuous human intervention rather than a one-time approval step.

Recommended organizational controls include disclosure of AI-assisted content and its limits; internal policies for data handling, validation, and quality assurance; clear examples of safe and unsafe use; proprietary or in-house systems where confidentiality is critical; and benchmarks for STE writing and translation. It connects these practices to the risk-based EU AI Act, UNESCO ethics principles, ISO standards, and OECD recommendations, but does not provide a detailed legal analysis.

## Analyst Takeaways

1. **Make the normative source explicit.** A model may propose language, but compliance is decided against the controlled-language standard.
2. **Treat AI assistance as a governed workflow, not a product feature.** Disclosure, validation, confidentiality, and accountability need named owners and records.
3. **Benchmark the actual controlled-language task.** General fluency is not evidence of terminology control or safety-critical correctness.
4. **Use local or proprietary deployment only as one safeguard.** It may reduce disclosure risk but does not establish accuracy or compliance.

## Questions and Limitations

- The paper is a short position statement, not an empirical comparison of tools or workflows.
- It names explainability and traceability requirements without specifying test protocols or acceptance thresholds.
- Regulatory references are high-level and will require jurisdiction- and use-case-specific interpretation.
- The claim that AI will not replace human authors is normative and predictive, not experimentally established.
