# AI-Induced Work Reallocation

Lowering the effective cost of an activity with AI changes **the composition of work, not just its speed**. A person may do more direct production, explore unfamiliar options, or obtain another functional perspective without involving a colleague. Assistance can therefore substitute for some coordination and access to others' expertise, while leaving accountability, retained knowledge, and durable relationships unresolved.

## Two Forms of Substitution

**Production versus coordination:** When assistance makes implementation or independent problem solving cheaper, workers can shift away from asking for help, reviewing, or managing contributions. An eligibility-threshold study of open-source maintainers found coding event share rising **5.4 percentage points** and project-management share falling **10.0 points** near the cutoff; controlled estimates were **+4.1 and −8.9 points**. Smaller repository peer groups and more unfamiliar repository and language exposure accompanied the shift. This is evidence of a changed activity portfolio, not a direct measurement of time saved or proof that displaced collaboration was unnecessary.

**Access to perspectives versus assembling colleagues:** Interactive assistance can offer alternative functional framings that a person can adapt using local context. In a cross-functional innovation experiment, assisted individuals produced proposals rated comparably to unaided pairs, and proposals became less tied to their authors' professional function. This supports partial substitution for collaborative functions in short-run ideation, **not formal equivalence, general team replacement, or possession of the colleague's expertise**.

These mechanisms need not operate identically. The first study estimates local effects of access eligibility on public activity; the second randomizes work configuration and assistance bundled with training. Neither isolates a universal causal mechanism of reduced coordination cost. Lower costs can also increase complementary activities rather than displace them, depending on how tasks fit together.

## Practical Use

Evaluate assistance as a work-design change rather than only a tool for accelerating an unchanged task list:

- **Map the displaced work.** Track production, review, coordination, maintenance, and exploration separately. Ask whether fewer interactions reflect reduced friction or lost checks, mentoring, and community exchange.
- **Distinguish artifact breadth from human capability.** A proposal covering multiple functions does not show retained expertise or independent detection of unfamiliar-domain errors. Keep consequential claims subject to knowledgeable review.
- **Measure the full resource budget.** Separate elapsed task time from person-hours, and public event shares from actual time allocation. More events, longer artifacts, or exposure to more languages do not establish better quality, proficiency, or wages.
- **Evaluate the portfolio as well as individual outputs.** Broader individual proposals can coexist with greater similarity across proposals. Check idea diversity, attention to existing projects, selection quality, and downstream implementation rather than assuming exploration improves organizational value.
- **Choose the comparison that matches the decision.** Replacing a colleague for a bounded task differs from improving an existing team or exceeding both a human and AI alone; see [Complementary Human–AI Performance](/vault/complementary-human-ai-performance.md).

## Limitations

The maintainer evidence is an intent-to-treat effect near a proprietary eligibility cutoff, not a universal effect of actually using assistance. Public events omit private work; repository peers are not an organizational headcount. Robustness analyses retain the direction of activity shifts but vary their magnitude, and sample accounting and event classification contain unresolved inconsistencies. Smaller peer groups do not establish layoffs or flatter hierarchies.

The teamwork evidence concerns one-day proposal work in one firm, not implementation success, sustained coordination, or retained cross-functional knowledge. The archived working paper has conflicting participant counts and timing magnitudes; comparable average ratings are not an equivalence test. Its assisted proposals are more semantically similar to one another, so improved individual breadth can come with homogenization. Lost peer learning and exchange are plausible tradeoffs to assess, not measured long-run harms in either study.
